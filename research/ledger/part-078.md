### Gameplay Loop
The core loop is built on cyclical, satisfying actions:
1. Inspecting the clutter: the player finds piles of tapes scattered along the aisles, under the counter, in return bins, and in delivery boxes.
2. Collection and transport: tapes can be picked up one by one, stacked in piles of up to 10 items, or loaded into a special wheeled cart with a capacity of up to 60 tapes.
3. Identification: the player examines the tape box, reads the vivid title, evaluates the cover, checks the color sticker on the spine, and the genre index.
4. Shelving: the tape is inserted into an empty slot on the appropriate genre rack. Upon correct placement, a soft audio click and a gentle controller vibration confirm success.
5. Filling sections: when a shelf is completely filled, a small neon light turns on above it, and the store's overall progress counter increases.

### Tools and Upgrades for the Clerk
As order is restored, the player unlocks useful items from the backroom:
* A metal cart on rubberized wheels, allowing quick transport of large batches of tapes without the risk of spilling them.
* A folding wooden stepladder, providing access to the top rows of tall shelving units.
* A belt-worn cassette player with a set of tapes, speeding up the player's walking pace and creating a rhythmic groove with upbeat music.
* An ultraviolet flashlight, highlighting markings on tape spines invisible to the naked eye for quick sorting in the dark.
* A faceted pencil, used for a mini-game to manually rewind loose magnetic tape on damaged cassettes.

### Four-player gameplay
In cooperative mode, four friends take on the roles of a night shift of losers:
1. Warehouse foreman: opens new supply crates, unpacks boxes, and sorts cassettes into primary sorting bins.
2. Cart driver: rushes around the sales floor with a cart, delivers sorted bins to the correct shelf aisles, risking tipping over on turns.
3. Stepladder climber: hauls a folding ladder around and neatly fills the upper, hard-to-reach tiers of shelving units.
4. Inspector auditor: roams the floor with a scanner, identifies cassettes that friends mistakenly put on the wrong shelves, rewinds chewed-up tapes with a pencil, and collects gold discs.

### Elements of friendly mischief
Co-op thrives on amusing interactions:
* Ability to throw cassettes at each other; hitting a teammate in the forehead knocks their stack of cassettes onto the floor with a loud sound.
* Watching found gold DVDs together: all four players can crowd into a cramped VIP booth, sit on small sofas, and watch video together on a CRT TV.
* Risk of collective failure: if someone clumsily knocks into a stepladder or pushes a cabinet with a cart, teammates risk triggering the collapse of an entire shelf.

### Technical risks and network limitations
* Synchronization of hundreds of physics objects: transmitting the coordinates of several hundred simultaneously moving cassettes over the network creates a heavy load on the network bandwidth. For the test, a model will be used where a cassette is physical only in hands or when falling, while on a shelf and in a cart it turns into a static object with a lightweight network identifier.
* Outcome of the research: if a closed test for 4 players demonstrates high virality and stable netcode without desync, co-op will go into the main release or a free update. If unresolvable technical issues arise, the game will remain an impeccably polished single-player project.
